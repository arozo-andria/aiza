1. Executive Summary & Evaluated Models

Malagasy administrative interactions are characterized by pervasive intra-sentential code-switching: citizens embed French bureaucratic terms into Malagasy syntax (e.g. "Very ny CIN-ko, aiza no manao déclaration de perte?"). This benchmark evaluates Intron Sahara alongside two open-source Malagasy fine-tunes on self-recorded authentic citizen queries in Antananarivo, Madagascar.

2. Dataset & Methodology

  • Corpus: 10 authentic administrative queries recorded via AIZA Studio (/record) covering lost IDs, birth certificates, marriage certificates, driver's licenses, address changes, passport fees, land titles, criminal records, and processing delays.
  • Linguistic Structure: 100% intra-sentential Malagasy/French code-switching.
  • Audio Specifications: 10 WebM/Opus recordings (01 to 10), 16kHz, total 62 seconds, 6.2s average length.
  • Ground Truth: Human-verified orthography normalized for Malagasy diacritics and French administrative terminology.

5. Qualitative Findings: The Code-Switching Asymmetry Paradox

Key Discovery: Entity Preservation Beats Carrier Syntax in Downstream Tasks
While WAXAL Whisper achieved the lowest acoustic WER (76.4%), Sahara achieved 3× higher downstream task matching (60% vs 20%). In administrative services, procedural intent is anchored in French terminology ("déclaration de perte", "certificat de naissance"). Because Sahara recognized French phrases accurately, AIZA was able to reliably match procedures even when the Malagasy prefix was distorted into French homophones. In contrast, monolingual Malagasy models phonetically broke French words (e.g. "déclaration de perte" → "kilarasyon-deo mperita"), completely missing the keywords.
Linguistic Failure Modes Observed:
  • Sahara (French mode): Map Malagasy syntax into French homophones ("Very ny CIN-ko" → "Ferens Yenne même aux"). Highly robust on French administrative compound nouns.
  • Whisper Malagasy fine-tunes: Accurately capture Malagasy grammatical particles ("tsy mety ny", "ho an'ny zanako"), but lack vocabulary for French loanwords, creating phonetic hallucinations.

6. Key Architecture Decisions & Ethics Note

Technical & Architectural Choices
  • Sahara as Primary Engine: Selected for lowest latency (3.2s) and high legal keyword retention in live PWA.
  • Mandatory Text Fallback: Given the 82.6% WER on Malagasy, text typing is a first-class citizen in AIZA's UI.
  • Deterministic Knowledge Grounding: Extracted intents query an immutable SQLite database (data/aiza.db). AIZA strictly abstains ("No verified procedure found") rather than fabricating legal advice.
Ethics, Safety & Inclusion
  • Informed Consent & Privacy: All audio clips were self-recorded with informed consent. Zero audio is stored or retained on disk by the deployed PWA.
  • Addressing Linguistic Inequity: Malagasy (30M speakers) is absent from AfriSwitch and major speech benchmarks. Surfacing this real-world gap is an intentional contribution of this project.

7. Reproducibility & Open Source

The benchmark script, raw results, model configurations, and self-recorded audio clips are fully published and reproducible:

./benchmark/venv/bin/python3 benchmark/run_benchmark.py
